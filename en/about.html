About the Author

A lens first, a story after

Andon Vey learned to look before he learned to tell. What a lens demands is what a story demands as well: choices, patience, and honesty about what stays inside the frame.

Andon Vey comes from a country that is flat and wet. Water there is something you pump away, guard, keep in check. Never something you look for. Perhaps that is why he writes about thirst.

He learned to look before he learned to tell. A lens forces you to choose: what falls inside the frame and what does not, and how long you dare to wait before you press the shutter. Anyone who looks that way discovers soon enough that a story asks exactly the same of you.

By day he works with systems that either hold or fail, with nothing in between. That is no bad schooling for a world you have to invent yourself. There too, every rule you forget comes back for you.

And then there is the question that will not let him go, for as long as he can remember. What is left of a person once others begin to retell their story? Who decides which part is kept, and who decides to stay silent?

He does not have the answer. He only needed a desert for it.
