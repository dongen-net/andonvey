The Question

One question will not let him go

Andon Vey writes about thirst, about power, and about the price every story asks of the one who lived it. At the centre of that writing sits a single question, asked again and again until a desert was the only place large enough for it.

The Central Question

What remains of a person once others begin to retell their story?

Who decides which part is kept, and who decides to stay silent?

Three Threads

How the question takes shape

Three themes braided through the trilogy. Each is asked of the world of Khalzam, where everything that makes life possible lies beneath the sand.

I

Water

On Khalzam, two suns hang in the sky, and water is counted, guarded, and weighed. Whoever holds the water holds what is possible. The trilogy asks what a society pays to keep a resource that scarce, and who is asked to pay it.

II

The Retold Life

A story outlives the one who lived it, and never completely. Across three volumes, the question recurs: which part of a person is kept, who decides it, and who makes the choice to stay silent.

III

The Unsaid Half

The prophecy has a second half that no one speaks aloud. Again and again, the tension of the story lives in what silence protects, and in the moment silence finally breaks.
